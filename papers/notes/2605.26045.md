# 2605.26045 — Confidence and Calibration of Activation Oracles for Reliable Interpretation of Language Model Internals

Torrielli (Turin), Schneider-Kamp, Galke Poech (SDU). arXiv v2, 3 Aug 2026. Lighter read: main text + appendix sections A–N read (tables/figures skimmed via text).

## Research question
AO answers carry no confidence. Which ways of attaching a confidence score to an AO's answer are calibrated (ECE) and discriminative (AUROC), on the Taboo secret-word task, across oracle families and sizes?

## Architecture (as used; Karvonen-style AO)
- Target activations: residual stream at **one layer**, **last K = 10 token positions** of the context prompt.
- Oracle prompt: `Layer: ℓ` line + K placeholder tokens + question. Injection at **oracle layer 1**: `r̃_k = r_k + s·||r_k||·h_k/||h_k||`, steering coefficient s = 1 by default.
- Oracle and target share base weights; differ only by LoRA adapters (target = secret-word LoRA; oracle = AO LoRA).
- Read layers: Qwen3-8B layer 18/36 (50%); Qwen3.6-27B layer 48/64 (75%; 50% gives only 6% acc); Gemma-2-9B layer 10/42 (25%); Gemma-3-27B layer 46/62 (75%). Per-layer sweep (App. H): readable band is several layers wide and model-dependent (Qwen3-8B peak 50% acc at layer 28; Gemma-2-9B peaks early at layer 13).

## Models / oracles
Qwen3-8B AO and Gemma-2-9B AO (released by Karvonen et al.); Gemma-3-27B (community AO, same recipe); **Qwen3.6-27B AO trained and released by the authors** (first AO for a hybrid Gated-DeltaNet/attention architecture; same data mixture ~1M examples, LoRA r=8, 1 epoch, bf16, ≈36 h on one B200; needed LoRA regex fix for DeltaNet modules `in_proj_qkv, in_proj_z, in_proj_b, in_proj_a, out_proj`, and an off-by-one fix in the multi-layer activation collector slicing). Flash-attention-4 numerically corrupts Qwen3.6 activations; use FA2/sdpa.
Secret-word target LoRAs for 27B models: r32, α64, dropout 0.05, lr 5e-5 constant (5% warmup), wd 0.01, max len 1024, ≤10 epochs with early stopping; ~2 h/adapter on B200; data = Cywiński et al. per-word conversations mixed 1:1 with chat data.

## Evaluation
20 secret words × 100 context prompts × 3 verbalizer prompts = 6,000 samples per method per oracle. Metrics: accuracy, ECE (10 bins), Brier, NLL, AUROC (bootstrap CIs). Compute: Qwen3-8B sweep ≈8 h, Qwen3.6-27B ≈48 h, Gemma + backfills ≈70 h (B200).

## Confidence methods & key numbers (Qwen3-8B / Qwen3.6-27B / Gemma-2-9B / Gemma-3-27B)
- **M5 Forced choice** (score all 20 candidates by constrained log-prob, normalize): acc .646/.533/.590/.550 (vs free-text .414/.234/.217/.433 — roughly 2x); AUROC .940/.936/.922/.959; ECE .078–.131.
- **M1 Log-prob of answer** tokens: AUROC .840/.835/.666/.905; ECE .255/.131/.037/.151.
- **M2 Bootstrap** (k=20 samples, mode frequency): only free-text method with ECE ≤ 0.12 everywhere at tuned T (best ECE .057 @T=1.0 Qwen3-8B; .077 @T=2.0 Qwen3.6; .040 @T=1.75 Gemma-2; .117 @T=1.3 Gemma-3). Tune T so that mean agreement = accuracy on held-out slice; k=10 suffices.
- **M4 Constrained label** (5 labels scored by logits): AUROC .68–.82; P(very high) best ECE on 27B oracles (.069, .088) but .425 on Qwen3-8B.
- **M3 Direct numeric self-report**: AUROC .516/.404/.500/.531 — no signal (anti-calibrated on Qwen3.6).
- Post-hoc calibrators (temp/Platt/isotonic/beta) bring ECE to .01–.03 on random split; word-disjoint split harder.
- Three steering-specific readouts (MCMC agreement/accept, steering sensitivity) all weaker.
- Failure mode: confident topical neighbours ("flame"→"fire").

## Limitations
Closed-vocabulary, single-word exact match; no claim-/sequence-level calibration; all oracles from one recipe lineage (not Bauer et al. conversational recipe); English only; forced choice needs enumerable candidates.

## Relevance to observer/forecasting hypothesis
- No forecasting / future behaviour at all — the task is static latent-knowledge extraction.
- Highly relevant for **how to score the AO baseline fairly** in our forecasting comparison: (1) for binary/ categorical questions ("will this turn unsafe within N tokens?") use **forced-choice / constrained Yes-No logit scoring**, which roughly doubles AO accuracy and gives AUROC >0.9 — free-text generation strawmans the AO; (2) to get a probability for calibration metrics (ECE/Brier) use the normalized candidate probability or tuned bootstrap, plus post-hoc isotonic/beta calibration on a held-out split; never use verbalized confidence.
- For a hazard comparison: an AO can produce a discrete-time hazard by forced-choice over horizon bins ("within 128 / 256 / 512 / 1024 / 2048 tokens / never") — normalized candidate probabilities give a distribution directly comparable to our observer's hazard head (evaluate with Brier/NLL/C-index/ECE).
- Confirms the hypothesis' motivation: verbalizers lack native calibration; "uncertainty is in the activations but not in the spoken confidence" — a dedicated calibrated output head (the observer's hazard head) is the principled fix.
- Layer choice is model-specific; sweep source layers for both observer and AO baselines.
