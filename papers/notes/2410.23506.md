# 2410.23506 — The Belief State Transformer (BST)
Hu, Ahn, Liu, Xu, Tomar, Langford, Teoh, Xu, Yan, Jayaraman, Lamb, Langford (MSR / UPenn / UT Austin). ICLR 2025. Read depth: MEDIUM (method, results, App. D–E).

## Research question
Can a transformer trained on both prefixes and suffixes (next-token prediction for the prefix + previous-token prediction for the suffix) learn a **compact belief state** that forward-only next-token or multi-token prediction cannot, and does this fix planning failures (star graph) and improve goal-conditioned generation?

## Method / architecture
- Forward encoder F(x_{1:t}) and backward encoder B(x_{t+k:T}) (GPT-2 style). The output head takes concat(F, B) and predicts the next token x_{t+1} (T_n) and the previous token x_{t+k-1} (T_p). The heads are tied except for the last layer (TinyStories: one 3-layer MLP, width 512, ReLU, with two outputs).
- **Loss (Eq. 2):** E_{t, x_{1:t}, k≤T−t}[ −log T_n(x_{t+1} | F(x_{1:t}), B(x_{t+k:T})) − log T_p(x_{t+k−1} | F(x_{1:t}), B(x_{t+k:T})) ], summed over all O(T²) valid prefix-suffix pairs (k≥2). For efficiency, all f_i and b_j are cached, head gradients are accumulated over pairs, and only then backpropagated into the encoders.
- **Improved practical version (Eq. 9):** adds a pure GPT head T_GPT(x_{t+1}|F) with weight γ, and λ to weight next vs. prev: E[ γ·(−log T_GPT) + (1−γ)( λ·(−log T_n) + (1−λ)·(−log T_p) ) ]. Encoders are shared, with a BERT-style segment embedding; 2% of pairs are subsampled; γ=0.9, λ=0.0.
- Inference: the suffix is empty, B(∅) is precomputed, and decoding is ordinary autoregressive, so there are no extra inference parameters. Optional extras: goal-conditioned planning (priority-queue rollouts scored by T_n given the goal suffix, Alg. 1/Eq. 7) and unconditional rollouts scored by the Prev head over the last k tokens (Eq. 8).
- Theory: Thm 2 says an ideal BST's forward encoding F(x_{1:t}) is a belief state, because one can sample the future backward with T_p plus B. Thms 3/4 say next-token and multi-token (independent per-offset heads T_j(F)=P(x_{t+j}|x_{1:t})) predictors do *not* guarantee belief states. Thm 1: after the "flawed cheat" in star graphs, learning the correct path is as hard as learning parity.

## Models
Small GPT-2-style models trained from scratch. TinyStories: BST encoders 8L/768d/8 heads (80M total), FIM baseline 12L/768d (85M). Improved-BST comparison: 57M encoder params. Single A100/H100.

## Datasets
- Star graphs G(2,5), G(5,5), G(2,20) (Bachmann & Nagarajan 2024); 10,000 evaluation graphs.
- TinyStories: 1k-token vocabulary, stories >256 tokens discarded, 2.7M stories, 1 epoch, batch size 256. Evaluation uses 100 held-out stories (first 50 tokens as prefix, last 100 tokens as suffix). No HF ID is given in the paper.

## Probe design
Small **MLP probes on the first hidden state** of G(2,5) star-graph sequences, each predicting the token at a specific future timestep (0→1 … 0→4), plus probes predicting graph-description tokens. Forward-only vs. BST representations are compared.

## Metrics
Star-graph path accuracy. GPT-4 pairwise judge win rate with binomial CI (100 stories × 6 judgments = 600 queries, order shuffled). Probe accuracy per future offset. Next/prev-token loss. Training iterations per second.

## Key numbers
- Star graph (Fig. 2): BST ≈100% on G(2,5) and G(2,20), ≈98% on G(5,5). Next-token/data augmentation/FIM stay near 1/d (≈50%, ≈50%, ≈10–18%). Multi-token: 100% on G(2,5), ≈0 on G(2,20) and G(5,5). Ablations (Fig. 4): removing the Prev objective or the backward encoder drops performance back to ≈1/d.
- **Future-token probes (Fig. 8):** from the first state, BST probes reach ≈1.0 accuracy for every future offset 0→1…0→4, while forward-only probes stay ≈0.2–0.25, close to the no-context baseline. Over training, information about **far-future tokens (end of sequence) becomes decodable first**, and nearer tokens later. In BST the far future is not harder to decode once trained (no horizon decay on this toy task). BST states also encode more of the graph description.
- TinyStories goal-conditioned: GPT-4 prefers BST over FIM 64% of the time (CI [54%, 74%]). Unconditional: BST ≈41% vs. FIM ≈28%, with ≈31% ties (read from figure). Ablations: beam search instead of Alg. 1, or Next-head scoring instead of Prev-head scoring, both lower the win rate.
- Table 1 (TinyStories): next-token loss GPT 0.929, original BST 1.013, improved BST 0.926. Speed: 3.086 vs. 0.205 vs. 0.801 it/s.

## Limitations
Small-scale proof of concept only (≤~114M params, toy graphs, TinyStories). O(T²) training cost, needing subsampling (still ≈4× slower than GPT). Evaluation relies on a GPT-4 judge. The original BST degrades next-token loss unless reweighted. Scaling to practical settings is left open. (NextLat 2511.05963 later reports BST is >3× slower than NextLat at 1.3B and fails on G_{7,7} with N=100 and fixed data.)

## Relevance to observer/forecasting hypothesis
- **Training signal from the future:** BST shows that conditioning a predictor on (prefix state, suffix encoding) gives the prefix state dense gradients about the whole future. For an observer, an analogous *training-only* backward/suffix encoder over future activation windows can shape the observer memory (e.g., a JEPA/CPC-style target encoder of future windows at several horizons). It can be dropped at inference, just as B(∅) is.
- **Evidence against per-offset independent heads:** Thm 4 says independent multi-token heads do not guarantee belief states. This argues for a recurrent or belief-state observer (component a) rather than a bank of independent "probe at horizon h" heads. The direct-verbalizer/probe baseline is close to this independent-head regime.
- **Probe protocol:** an MLP probe from one early state to a token at a specific future offset, tracked across training, is a clean recipe for measuring predictability vs. lead distance. The "far future learned first" observation is a hint that end-of-sequence attributes (e.g., the final outcome or whether a response ends unsafe) may be easier targets than intermediate tokens.
- **Goal/outcome scoring:** the Prev-head scoring of endings (Eq. 8) resembles scoring whether a candidate continuation reaches a goal state. This could inspire an observer head that scores the likelihood of a target event given the current belief state, though BST does not provide hazard or event-time formulations.
- Not applicable: BST requires training the LM itself, and there are no hazard losses, censoring, or long (100–2048-token) horizons.
