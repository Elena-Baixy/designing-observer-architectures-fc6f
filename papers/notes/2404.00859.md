# 2404.00859 — Do Language Models Plan Ahead for Future Tokens? (Wu, Morris, Levine; COLM 2024)

## Research question
Hidden states at step t carry information used at t+tau. Is this because the model deliberately computes future-useful features at a cost to the present ("pre-caching"), or because the features useful now are also useful later ("breadcrumbs")?

## Method
- **Gradient decomposition.** With tied weights, the gradient is an upper-triangular sum: grad = sum_i sum_{j>=i} d l_j / d theta_i. The off-diagonal terms (i < j) are the training signal for pre-caching.
- **Myopic descent.** Drop the off-diagonal terms: the past keys/values are computed from a *cloned and detached* copy of the previous hidden states, so the loss at position j sends no gradient to past positions.
  - Tied-myopic parameters: theta~ in argmin_theta sum_i l_i(theta~, ..., theta~, theta) (Def. 3).
  - Theorem 13: under forward-bias, strong convexity and smoothness, myopic descent converges to these parameters.
- **Myopia gap.** Loss(myopic) - Loss(vanilla) measures how much pre-caching matters.
- **Local myopia bonus.** Train a new model whose past K/V come from a frozen vanilla model. This measures how much the vanilla model sacrifices the present for the future.
- **Baseline: "transformer bigram"** (past K/V zeroed out).

## Models
- Synthetic: 2-layer transformer, d = 128.
- Natural language: GPT-2 124M architecture trained from scratch.
- Scaling: Pythia 14M to 2.8B, fine-tuned from public checkpoints (vanilla vs. myopic).
- Multiplication: GPT-2-small from scratch.

## Datasets
- Synthetic D_p: x_n ~ N(0,1), z_n ~ Ber(p), y_n = z_n * sum_{i=1..10} sin(10 x_{n-i}) + (1 - z_n) x_n, with N = 64. Smaller p means the cached feature is needed further in the future (or never).
- GPT-2: MS MARCO, 4.6M sequences truncated to 64 tokens, one epoch. Per-position loss measured on a PG-19 sliding window (100K tokens).
- Pythia: 10M sequences of 64 tokens subsampled from the Pile.
- Evaluation benchmarks: LAMBADA, PIQA, SciQ, ARC-Easy.
- Multiplication: up to 8 digits, reversed and zero-padded; 10M examples.

## Probe design
- Synthetic setting only: linear regression from each layer's hidden state at position n to sin(b x_{n-i}) for each i, reporting in-sample R^2 on 50k sequences. Per-neuron correlations are also reported.
- Probe validity: inputs have near-zero linear correlation with the target (rho < 1e-20), so the probe cannot compute the target itself.

## Metrics
Normalized Huber loss L/p (synthetic), validation cross-entropy, per-position CE, benchmark accuracy, multiplication exact-match.

## Key numbers
- **Synthetic** (L/p, vanilla vs. myopic):
  - p = 0.01: 0.096 vs. 1.10. p = 0.1: 0.016 vs. 0.97. p = 0.3: 0.0030 vs. 1.03. p = 1: 0.0074 vs. 1.26 (trivial zero predictor: 1.26).
  - The vanilla model clearly pre-caches. Layer 1 encodes sin(b x_n) (R^2 up to ~0.9+). Layer 2 encodes sin(b x_{n-i}) for 0 <= i <= 10, mainly in a single 1-d subspace storing the sum.
  - Pre-caching appears even when p = 0.01, i.e. when the feature is rarely ever used.
- **GPT-2 CE:** vanilla 3.28, myopic 3.40, local myopic 3.26, transformer bigram 5.33.
  - Myopia gap = 0.12 nats; local myopia bonus = 0.02. This points mostly to breadcrumbs.
  - The per-position gap is negative (~-0.10) at position 0. It rises quickly to ~+0.06 by ~5 tokens and to ~+0.11 by position 60, so it grows with context length.
- **Pythia scaling:** from 70M up, the CE myopia gap grows with parameter count. Benchmark gaps widen at scale (e.g. LAMBADA ~0.58 vanilla vs. ~0.40 myopic at 2.8B; ARC-Easy ~0.65 vs. ~0.56).
- **Multiplication / filler tokens:** vanilla models improve with extra zero-padding; myopic models get worse. Pre-caching is what lets models use filler-token computation.

## Limitations
- Natural-language contexts are only 64 tokens, so there is no evidence about 100–2000-token horizons.
- Models are small (up to 2.8B), and Pythia is only fine-tuned, not pretrained, myopically.
- Pre-caching is measured through loss gaps, not by decoding what is cached.
- Convergence theory needs strong assumptions (convexity, forward bias).

## Relevance to observer/forecasting hypothesis
- **Theoretical grounding for why future info sits in activations.** For small models it is mostly "breadcrumbs" (features useful now that are also predictive later). Deliberate planning grows with scale. An observer should therefore expect larger long-horizon gains on bigger subject models.
- **Breadcrumbs imply future information is diffuse.** It may be scattered across positions rather than packed into one future-designated direction at a single token. That argues for an observer with **trajectory memory** (aggregating across positions) over a single-position verbalizer/probe. The myopia gap growing with context length is consistent with this.
- **A borrowable diagnostic.** A myopic subject model (stop-gradient to the past) is a control: the observer's long-lead advantage should shrink on it if the advantage comes from pre-cached plans.
- **Probe hygiene.** Choose targets with no linear relation to the inputs, so that a probe's R^2 reflects encoded information rather than computation done by the probe.
- No survival, hazard or CPC components here.
