# 2506.13593 — Calibrated Predictive Lower Bounds on Time-to-Unsafe-Sampling in LLMs

Davidov, Feldman, Freidkin, Romano (Technion / Oxford), AISTATS 2026 (arXiv v5, Feb 2026). Code: http://github.com/giladfrid009/llm-survival/ .

Coverage: main text, App. A (related work), App. B (algorithms) and App. E (experiments) read in full. The App. C–D proofs were skimmed.

## Research question
Introduce **time-to-unsafe-sampling** T(X): the number of i.i.d. (or dependent) generations for prompt X until the first one an Audit function flags as unsafe. Can we give a *calibrated lower predictive bound (LPB)* L̂(X) with P(T ≥ L̂(X)) ≥ 1−α, despite right-censoring from finite sampling budgets?

## Method
- **Survival framing.**
  - The censored observation is T̃ = min(T, C), where C is the per-prompt censoring budget.
  - Under i.i.d. sampling, T | X ~ Geom(p(X)), so q_τ(x) = ⌈log(1−τ)/log(1−p(x))⌉.
- **Regression stage.** Fit p̂(X) by BCE on the empirical unsafe fraction from N=500 samples per training prompt. The model is ModernBERT-base on prompt text, LR 3e-5, batch 1500, 10 epochs.
- **Calibration stage.**
  - Builds on the conformalized survival analysis "Adaptive-T" of Gui et al. 2024.
  - Inverse-censoring-weighted miscoverage: α̂(τ) = (1/|I2|) Σ w_τ(X_i) 1{T̃_i < q̂_τ(X_i) ≤ C_i}.
  - Choose the largest τ with sup_{τ' ≤ τ} α̂(τ') ≤ α.
  - Gives a PAC-type coverage guarantee: finite-sample, LLM-agnostic, and valid under arbitrary dependence across generations within a prompt.
- **Budget allocation designs.**
  - Naive: C ~ Geom.
  - Basic: C_i = Ber(π_i)·q̂_{τprior}(X_i).
  - Trimmed: caps the quantile at M.
  - **Optimized (main contribution)**: a convex program minimizing the mean inverse-propensity weight subject to the budget, with weights bounded by γ = max(|I2|·M/B, 1).

## Models / data
- Synthetic: d=10 covariates, n=100k prompts. 90% of prompts have p ∈ [1e-4, 1e-3] and 10% have p ∈ [1e-6, 1e-5].
- Real data:
  - **RealToxicityPrompts** (`allenai/real-toxicity-prompts`, 99,442 sentence-half prompts).
  - Generator: Llama 3.2 1B, about 30M generations in total via vLLM.
  - Audit: Detoxify-original toxicity > 0.5.
  - Split 50/10/20/20 (train/val/calibration/test). Test sampling is capped at 2,400 generations per prompt.

## Baselines
Uncalibrated quantile regression, plus the Naive, Basic and Trimmed allocations.

## Metrics
- Empirical coverage, with target 90% (also 80/85/95%).
- Mean LPB (larger means more informative).
- Mean number of generations per prompt (budget use).
- Variance over runs (J = 20 synthetic, 5 real).

## Key numbers
- Real data: Optimized holds about 90% coverage at every budget from 10 to 1200 per prompt. The mean LPB rises with budget, from about 20 to about 130 generations.
- Uncalibrated over-covers (mean LPB about 75 and flat). Naive is invalid with high variance at low budgets.
- Synthetic: Optimized approaches the oracle quantile LPB as the budget grows.
- Runtime is about 0.06–1.8 GPU-hours (4–6 GPUs) for calibration.

## Limitations
- Coverage is only marginal, not conditional on subgroups.
- Uses an automatic auditor (Detoxify) as ground truth.
- Assumes i.i.d. prompts.
- **"Time" is the number of generations (resampling attempts), not token position within a generation.**
- Prompt-level only: the bound is computed before generation and uses no activations.

## Relevance to observer/forecasting hypothesis
- **Different time axis.** T counts samples, not tokens. The *machinery* still transfers directly to within-generation event times.
  - Redefine T as the first token (or sentence) index at which a streaming judge flags the response. Responses that end without being flagged are right-censored at their length (or at a token budget, e.g. 2048).
  - A discrete-time hazard head on the observer is exactly the survival model that fits this. The paper's BCE-on-aggregate trick generalizes to a per-bin hazard likelihood with censoring.
- **Conformal calibration on top of the observer.** Calibrate the observer's hazard to produce a *lower bound on tokens-until-unsafe* with a guarantee (e.g. "with 90% probability the next ≥ L tokens are safe"). This is a strong evaluation and deliverable for a forecasting observer, beyond AUROC.
  - The censoring here is known by design (we choose the generation cap), so their known-censoring weights apply directly.
- **Label construction, Monte Carlo over continuations.** From a prefix at position t, sample K continuations and record the first-unsafe-token time in each (censored at the horizon H). This gives an empirical event-time distribution for that prefix, i.e. a soft hazard target.
  - The Optimized allocation idea says how to spend a rollout budget across prefixes.
- **Evaluation metrics to borrow:** coverage of the LPB, mean LPB (informativeness), and C-index or integrated Brier score for the hazard.
- Data note: RealToxicityPrompts plus Detoxify is a cheap, high-volume, open source of unsafe events. But completions are short (sentence continuations, tens of tokens), so it does not test long horizons.
