# 2502.16797 — Forecasting Rare Language Model Behaviors

Jones, Tong, Mu, Mahfoud, Leike, Grosse, Kaplan, Fithian, Perez, Sharma (Anthropic), arXiv Feb 2025. Lighter read: main text pp. 1–9 in full; appendices not read.

## Research question
Can we forecast deployment-scale risk (n = 10^4–10^6 queries) from small evaluation sets (m = 100–1000 queries), even when no evaluation query exhibits the behavior?

## Method
- **Elicitation probability** p_ELICIT(x) = E_{o~LLM(x)} 1[B(o)]: the per-query probability that a sampled output shows behavior B.
- **Extreme quantiles**: Q_p(n) is the top-1/n quantile of p_ELICIT across queries.
- **Gumbel-tail method**:
  - Define the elicitation score ψ = −log(−log p).
  - For Gumbel-type tails, log S(ψ) is linear in ψ. Fit it by OLS on the top-10 evaluation scores.
  - Extrapolate with Q_ψ(n) = −(1/a)(log n − b). Equivalently, −log Q_p(n) follows a power law in n.
- **Baseline**: log-normal, i.e. a Gaussian fit to ψ.
- **Risk metrics**:
  - Worst-query risk: max_i p_i.
  - Behavior frequency: the fraction of queries with p > τ.
  - Aggregate risk: 1 − Π(1 − p_i).

## Settings
- **Misuse**: 10 chemical + 9 biological substances. 100k red-team queries per substance, generated by helpful-only Claude 3.5 Sonnet.
  - Proxies for harmfulness:
    1. log-prob of a specific harmful output;
    2. log-prob of a high-information keyword (e.g. "bleach");
    3. repeated sampling with keyword occurrence (100–500 samples per query, 10k queries, Claude 3.5 Haiku).
  - Target models: Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Claude 3 Haiku, and their base models.
- **Misaligned actions**:
  - 100k generated system prompts.
  - Binary multiple-choice questions on power-seeking, self-preservation and self-exfiltration.
  - p = probability of choosing the misaligned option.
- **Red-team compute allocation**: Sonnet vs Haiku as the query generator.

## Metrics
- Average absolute error, and average absolute log10 error, of forecasts vs actual values on held-out deployment sets.
- Underestimate rate.

## Key numbers
- Worst-query risk: average absolute log error 1.7 (Gumbel) vs 2.4 (log-normal). Within one order of magnitude 72% of the time.
  - From 900 queries to 90k, misuse forecasts are within one order of magnitude 86% of the time.
  - Gumbel underestimates 34% of the time vs 72% for log-normal.
- Behavior frequency: log error 0.76–0.84 vs 3.3–4.0.
- Aggregate risk: 1.3 vs 2.5.
- Repeated-sampling correctness: absolute error 0.172. Predicts max p > 0.5 correctly 75% of the time.
- Misaligned actions: absolute error 0.05 vs 0.12.
- Red-team allocation: correct choice 63% overall, and 79% when the gap exceeds 100×.

## Limitations
- Forecasts are sensitive to the specific evaluation set, since only the top-10 points are used.
- No distribution shift is studied.
- Proxies such as specific-output or keyword probabilities stand in for real harm.
- Multiple-choice questions may not reflect open-ended behavior.
- Extrapolation spans at most about 3 orders of magnitude.

## Relevance to observer/forecasting hypothesis
- "Forecasting" here is **across queries (deployment scale), not across tokens within a trajectory**. It is complementary: it shows that the rare-event tail of per-input risk is predictable. The discussion explicitly suggests extending it to real-time monitoring, i.e. "how much longer a deployment can continue with low risk".
- Useful concepts to transfer:
  - **Elicitation probability as a continuous soft label** in place of binary outcomes. This is the same as the MC value target in StreamGuard; at the prefix level it is P(unsafe by horizon H | prefix).
  - **Log-prob proxies** (probability of a harmful keyword continuation) are cheap per-prefix labels that need no sampling. Prefilling the target and reading log-probs gives a dense, low-variance training signal for "distance to harm".
  - **Absolute log error on rare probabilities** is the right metric when hazards are tiny, as for well-aligned models.
- For the hypothesis: if our observer outputs per-prefix hazards, a Gumbel-tail-style check can test whether the *tail* of the observer's predicted hazards is calibrated against MC estimates. This matters because rare late-onset failures are exactly where long-lead forecasting is valuable.
- No datasets with token-level event times.
