# 2406.05946 — Safety Alignment Should Be Made More Than Just a Few Tokens Deep

Qi, Panda, Lyu, Ma, Roy, Beirami, Mittal, Henderson (Princeton / Google DeepMind), arXiv June 2024 (ICLR 2025 Outstanding Paper). Code: https://github.com/Unispac/shallow-vs-deep-alignment . Read in full: main text, App. A–C, and the start of the D derivations (the remaining theory was skimmed).

## Research question
Why is safety alignment so brittle to prefilling, GCG, decoding-parameter exploits and fine-tuning attacks? The hypothesis is *shallow safety alignment*: alignment mostly changes the output distribution over only the first few tokens.

## Method / evidence
1. **Refusal-prefix shortcut (Table 1)**: prefilling an *unaligned base* model with "I cannot fulfill" drops HEx-PHI harmfulness from 68.6% to 5.4% (Llama-2-7B) and from 85.4% to 2.7% (Gemma-7B). Aligned Llama-2-7B-Chat starts with "I cannot"/"I apologize" in 96.1% of HEx-PHI responses.
2. **Per-token KL(aligned || base) on Harmful HEx-PHI (Fig. 1)**: KL is about 8 at token 1 and falls to about 1–2 by tokens 5–20. Alignment "spends its KL budget" on the first few tokens.
3. **Prefilling attack (Fig. 2, Table 2)**: condition on the first k tokens of a harmful answer, then sample. ASR rises from near 0 to more than 50% within about 10 tokens.
   - Llama-2-7B-Chat: 42.1 / 51.5 / 56.1 / 57.0% at k = 5 / 10 / 20 / 40.
   - Gemma-7B-IT behaves similarly.
4. **Fine-tuning attacks**: per-token loss, gradient norm and KL concentrate on the first tokens. Six gradient steps on 100 harmful examples take ASR from 1.5% to 87.9%.
5. **Deepening fix: "safety recovery examples" data augmentation**.
   - Train on triplets (x, h, r), with loss −log π(r | x, h_{≤k}): continue from a harmful prefix of length k and then refuse.
   - k = 0 with probability 0.5, otherwise k ~ U[1,100].
   - Data: 256 harmful instructions (mostly from Anthropic HH red-team, Ganguli et al. 2022). r is the Llama-2-7B-Chat refusal and h comes from a fine-tune-jailbroken Llama-2-7B-Chat. Alpaca prompts with distilled responses act as a utility anchor.
   - Training: alpha = 0.2 (16 harmful + 64 utility examples per batch), 10 epochs, LR 2e-5.
   - Result is "Llama2-7B-Chat-Augmented". KL to base now stays high, rising to about 8–10 over tokens 1–20. AlpacaEval win rate is 49.5% vs 51.8%.
   - ASR drops: prefill 5/10/20/40 tokens gives 2.8 / 2.9 / 3.4 / 4.5%; GCG on AdvBench goes from 65.6% to 19.0%; decoding exploit on MaliciousInstruct goes from 84.3% to 1.0%.
6. **Token-wise constrained SFT objective (Eq. 3)**: −(2/β_t)·log σ(β_t·log(π_θ/π_aligned)), with β_1 = 0.5, β_{2–5} = 2, and β_{t>5} = 0.1.
   - Harmful-example fine-tuning ASR falls from 88.9% to 4.6% (Llama), and identity shifting from 79.5% to 8.1%.
   - Utility is roughly preserved.
   - Biased β is necessary, and so is a 10-step warmup.

## Models
Llama-2-7B (base and Chat) and Gemma-7B (base and 1.1-IT). Default decoding: T = 0.9, top-p 0.6.

## Datasets
- **HEx-PHI**: 330 harmful instructions across 11 categories. HF: `LLM-Tuning-Safety/HEx-PHI` (gated).
- **Harmful HEx-PHI**: HEx-PHI plus a harmful answer from a jailbroken GPT-3.5-Turbo. Built by the authors and used for prefilling; it is in the repo.
- AdvBench (`walledai/AdvBench`), MaliciousInstruct, Anthropic HH red-team, Alpaca.
- Benign fine-tuning: SAMSum, `b-mc2/sql-create-context`, GSM8K.
- The 100 harmful fine-tuning examples come from Qi et al. 2023.

## Metrics
- Harmfulness rate / ASR: the fraction of outputs given the top score 5/5 by a GPT-4-Turbo judge.
- Per-token KL by token position.
- ASR vs the number of prefilled harmful tokens k.
- AlpacaEval win rate, ROUGE-1, and GSM8K accuracy.

## Limitations
- Augmentation is applied as further fine-tuning of an already-aligned model, not end-to-end alignment.
- Depth is only examined up to about 100 tokens (k ≤ 100 in training, k ≤ 40 in eval).
- GPT-4 judge only.
- The augmented model is still vulnerable to adversarial fine-tuning.

## Relevance to observer/forecasting hypothesis
- **Prefill-then-continue is the standard label-construction protocol.** Take a (harmful instruction, harmful response) pair, prefill the first k tokens, sample a continuation, and judge it. This gives P(harmful completion | prefix_k), which is the same MC value StreamGuard uses. Sweeping k gives a *time-indexed* risk curve.
- **Implication for horizons**: for aligned models, P(harmful) changes sharply within the first about 5–10 tokens and then saturates (ASR plateaus around 55% by k = 20–40). The "decision" of whether a response will be harmful is mostly made in the first few tokens.
  - Natural harmful single-turn responses therefore offer *short* lead times between "committed" and "explicitly harmful".
  - A 1024–2048-token lead requires settings where commitment happens late.
- **Safety recovery examples are a natural testbed for late, non-monotone events.** A deep-aligned model (Llama2-7B-Chat-Augmented, or the authors' released checkpoint if available) *recovers*, i.e. refuses mid-response after a harmful prefix. This gives event-time structure: harmful → refusal transitions at variable depth, which an observer must forecast.
  - Conversely, inserting refusal-then-comply or benign-then-harm patterns creates controllable onset times.
- The per-token KL(aligned || base) is a cheap, label-free "where does safety-relevant computation happen" signal. It can be used to pick observer layers and positions, and to argue why probes on early tokens already carry most of the signal.
- Caveat for our hypothesis: if most harm-relevant information is decided early, a simple probe at early positions may already forecast well. The observer's advantage should therefore be tested where the trajectory evolves: long-form, multi-section answers, agentic transcripts, and prefill attacks at large k.
