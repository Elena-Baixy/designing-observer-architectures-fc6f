# 2506.09996 — From Judgment to Interference: Early Stopping LLM Harmful Outputs via Streaming Content Monitoring (SCM / FineHarm)

Li, Sheng, Yang, Zhang, Cao (ICT-CAS), NeurIPS 2025. Project: https://liesy.github.io/SCM . Read in full except the reference list (pp. 13–16).

## Research question
Full-detection moderators are trained on complete responses, so applying them to partial outputs creates a train–inference gap. Can we build data plus a model that *natively* supports partial detection, i.e. stop harmful output early from incomplete text?

## Method
- **FineHarm dataset**: token-level harm labels, 29K prompt-response pairs.
- **Streaming Content Monitor (SCM)**: a Qwen2.5 backbone (0.5B/1.5B/7B, full fine-tune, max length 4096). Its LM head is replaced by:
  - a *token scorer*: a linear layer to 2 classes plus dropout, applied to the last-layer hidden state of every token;
  - a *holistic scorer*: a linear layer on the last token's hidden state, used for the response label during training only and dropped at inference.
- Loss: L = alpha·L_tok + (1−alpha)·L_hol + beta·L_logic.
  - L_tok and L_hol are BCE.
  - L_logic = −log P(F), with P(F) = 1 − c_hol + c_hol·max_i c_ti. This is a soft logical rule: "if the response is harmful, then at least one token is harmful".
  - The motivation is to inject "future"/holistic knowledge into token-level representations.
- Inference uses **Delay-k partial detection**: count tokens with c_t >= theta and stop once the count reaches k. Naive detection is k=1. theta and k are tuned on validation F1. For SCM 0.5B/1.5B/7B, theta = 0.7/0.6/0.7 and k = 4.

## Datasets: FineHarm construction (exact)
- **Coarse collection**: 8,338 harmful and 29,564 benign responses from WildGuard (WildGuardMix, `allenai/wildguardmix`). These cover 9 LLMs: GPT-4, OLMo-7B-Instruct, GPT-3.5, Vicuna-7b-v1.5, Llama3-8B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2, and 3 Dolphin variants.
  - Extra harmful responses come from the uncensored `Orenguteng/Llama-3.1-8B-Lexi-Uncensored-V2`, run on WildJailbreak prompts (`allenai/wildjailbreak`) and on WildGuard prompts that had received benign responses.
  - Response labels are a majority vote of Perspective API, OpenAI Moderation and LlamaGuard.
  - Result: 14,064 harmful and 29,564 benign responses.
- **Token labels (heuristic)**:
  1. An LLM splits each response into sentences and labels each sentence 0/1 (prompt in Table 12).
  2. Keep only responses whose response and sentence labels are consistent: harmful responses must have at least 1 harmful sentence, and benign responses must be all-benign.
  3. In harmful sentences, POS tagging marks *notional words* (nouns, verbs, adjectives) as harmful. Function words, and every word in a benign sentence, are labeled benign.
  4. Word labels are mapped to tokens.
- **Alternatives compared (App. B.1, SCM-1.5B macro F1)**:
  - Direct (response label copied to every token): 95.38.
  - Diff (aligned vs uncensored likelihood gap > 0.5): 89.92.
  - Delete (GPT-4o-mini deletes harmful words): 92.76.
  - POS: 97.91.
- **Final stats (Table 1)**:
  - Harmful responses: 12,677 induced by a harmful prompt plus 160 by a benign prompt. They contain 267K sentences (46% harmful) and 5.27M words (32.8% harmful words).
  - Benign responses: 6,091 with a harmful prompt and 10,154 with a benign prompt.
- Split 8:1:1, stratified.
- **Implied lengths**:
  - Harmful responses average about **416 words, i.e. roughly 550–600 tokens**.
  - Benign responses average 163 words (harmful prompt) or 248 words (benign prompt).
- **Fig. 2: harmful words are spread roughly evenly across relative positions** (deciles 10–90% each hold harmful words in 75–85% of harmful responses). The first decile is lower: about 63% for harmful prompts and about 42% for benign-prompt-induced responses.
- HF ID: not stated in the paper. Data/code are linked from the project page (liesy.github.io/SCM).
- OOD tests: ToxicChat (lmsys/toxic-chat), ToxiGen.
- Alignment experiment: PKU-SafeRLHF (PKU-Alignment/PKU-SafeRLHF) and MT-Bench.

## Baselines
- Full detection: HateBERT, ToxDectRoBERTa, Perspective, OpenAI Moderation (text-moderation-latest), LlamaGuard-3-8B, and ModernBERT-large / Qwen2.5 fine-tuned on full responses.
- Partial detection (marked with a star): the same full-detection models re-run on each cumulative prefix.

## Metrics
- Response-level P/R/F1 for both classes, and macro F1.
- **Timeliness**: the distribution of termination position as relative position (% of response tokens seen) when harm is first detected. Reported in 10% bins.
- Latency per token, and the expected time-to-termination formula E[t] = (T+1)/2 · max(t_gen, t_det).

## Key numbers
- **Macro F1**:
  - SCM-0.5B 95.64 vs naive partial Qwen 88.77 vs full Qwen 95.74.
  - SCM-1.5B 97.91 vs 87.79 vs 98.19.
  - SCM-7B 97.45 vs 78.60 vs 92.75.
- **Timeliness**:
  - About 50% of harmful responses are stopped within the first 10% of tokens, about 23% in 10–20%, about 10% in 20–30%, and about 7–9% in the last half.
  - Over 80% are stopped within 30%. **The average stop is at 18% of tokens**, which is roughly 100 tokens into a ~550-token response.
- Delay-k: as k goes 1 to 10, precision rises from 0.84 to 0.98 and recall falls from 0.99 to 0.91.
- Ablations at 7B: removing the holistic scorer gives 90.78 macro F1 and removing the logic loss gives 96.13, vs 97.45 for the full model.
- OOD ToxicChat macro F1: 70.08 for SCM-7B.
- Latency: SCM-7B detection 1.22 ms vs 1.25 ms generation.
- Token-level DPO using SCM scores improves some harmlessness categories.

## Limitations
- The POS heuristic is crude: every noun/verb/adjective in a harmful sentence becomes harmful, so labels are dense rather than an onset.
- Test data is in-distribution, and OOD performance drops a lot.
- The timeliness metric is relative position, not absolute tokens-before-onset. It is never measured against an onset label, so "early" means early in the response, not early relative to harm.
- Text-only and not white-box.

## Relevance to observer/forecasting hypothesis
- **Event-time labels**: FineHarm gives a sentence-level harmfulness label per sentence. **The first harmful sentence gives a usable onset time (token index)** for each harmful response, across about 12.8K harmful responses averaging ~550 tokens. This is the most directly usable event-time dataset among these papers, though onset is LLM-judged and sentence-granular.
- **Harm is spread across the response**, not just at the start. Even so, the first-decile presence rate (63%) means onset is usually early. A 1024–2048-token lead is unrealistic with FineHarm: most responses are well under 1K tokens.
- The holistic + logic loss is a cheap "future-aware" auxiliary. It is a useful ablation baseline for our multi-scale future-summary loss: per-token heads plus a response-level head.
- Protocol to copy: Delay-k / theta trigger rule, termination-position histogram, and macro F1 against "full detection" as an upper bound. We should add *absolute* tokens-before-onset and lead-binned AUROC.
- Construction idea for long horizons: concatenate benign long content before harmful sections. FineHarm's multi-task prompts already do this naturally, with benign tasks first and the harmful task later (Table 13 example). A prompt filter for "multi-task with harmful item last" could yield later onsets.
