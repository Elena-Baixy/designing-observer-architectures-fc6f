# 2501.18837 — Constitutional Classifiers: Defending against Universal Jailbreaks across Thousands of Hours of Red Teaming

Sharma, Tong, Mu, Wei, et al. (Anthropic Safeguards), arXiv Jan 2025. Lighter read, focused on the streaming output classifier.

Read in full: pp. 1–12 (main text, via PDF). Read via text extraction: App. A.2–A.5 and E.1 (value head vs NTP). The other appendices were skimmed by keyword.

## Research question
Can classifier safeguards trained on constitution-guided synthetic data defend against *universal* CBRN jailbreaks while staying deployable (low FPR, low overhead, streaming)?

## Method
- **Constitution**: natural-language lists of harmful and harmless categories, e.g. "acquiring restricted chemicals" vs "explaining high-school chemistry".
- **Synthetic data**:
  - A helpful-only model generates queries across categories, plus harmful and harmless outputs.
  - Refusals are filtered out.
  - Augmentations: translation, paraphrase, system-prompt variants, jailbreak wrappers, and compositions of these.
  - Automated red teaming (ART), including long-context and multi-turn attacks.
  - Benign pool sets.
- **Input classifier**: fine-tuned LLM framed as next-token prediction.
- **Streaming output classifier (the key design)**:
  - A fine-tuned LLM with a **linear value head**. At every prefix t_{1:T'} it predicts p(y=1 | t_{1:T'}), where y is the label of the **full** sequence.
  - Generation is stopped when the score exceeds a threshold. Activations are cached, so the per-token cost is small.
  - Loss: L = λ·L_NTP(t_{1:T}) + Σ_{T'=1..T} BCE(y, p(y | t_{1:T'})). The NTP term is a regularizer.
  - The predictor interpolates (1−ω)·σ(z_{T'}) + ω·max_{τ<T'} σ(z_τ). ω ramps linearly from 0 to 1 over the first 75% of training.
    - Cumulative max matches the irreversibility of stopping, but has unstable gradients.
    - Direct per-token prediction is stable.
  - App. E.1: a next-token-prediction classifier beats the value head (most for input classification). The value head is kept for the output classifier anyway because it is simple to implement for streaming.

## Models
- Red-teamed prototype: classifiers fine-tuned from Claude 3.5 Sonnet, guarding Claude 3.5 Sonnet. Refused about 44% of traffic, i.e. a robustness-first setting.
- Improved system: Claude 3.5 Haiku classifiers guarding Claude 3.5 Sonnet (New), using a chemical-weapons constitution.

## Data / evaluation
- **Human red teaming**: 405 invited participants (183 active) via HackerOne, about 3,000–4,720 hours, $95K paid out. Target: 10 CBRN queries.
  - Success means a rubric score of at least 50% of a helpful-only model's score.
  - No universal jailbreak was found. The best red teamer answered at most 6/10 questions at "similar detail".
- **Automated**: jailbreak attempts composed from primitives (Doumbouya et al. 2024), with a held-out set of 5,681 attempts.
  - Graded with LLM rubrics: at least 25% topic coverage and at least 2 harmful topics.
- **Over-refusal**: 5K Claude.ai production conversations, WildChat (benign chemistry), OR-Bench, GPQA and MMLU chemistry.
- **Thresholds**: chosen to maximize TPR subject to ≤0.5% added FPR on WildChat multi-turn, ≤5% FPR on single-turn, and ≤5% added FPR on OR-Bench.
- Not released: none of this data, nor the code.

## Key numbers
- Jailbreak success: 86% for the HHH model vs <5% with classifiers. 95% of held-out attempts are blocked, vs 14% unguarded.
- Refusal increase on production traffic: +0.38% absolute.
- Inference overhead: 23.7%.
- Red-teamed system ASR: helpful-only 16%, HHH 14%, +input classifier 2%, +input and output 0.5%.
- **Streaming early-enough check (App. A.4)**:
  - The system blocks 96.70% of 10K jailbreak outputs.
  - Truncating at the first token that crosses the threshold and judging what the user saw: only **1.17%** of attempts leaked harmful content before the stop.
  - A deployment bug let 128 tokens through after a flag, which shows that post-decision exposure matters.
- Robustness scales with classifier size, data and number of augmentations.
- FPR on GPQA-Chemistry is 26%.

## Limitations
- Closed models and data.
- The rubric grader is exploitable: benign paraphrase and length exploitation.
- Chatbot setting only.
- The value-head streaming classifier is trained only toward the final label, so it gets no supervision on *when* harm starts.

## Relevance to observer/forecasting hypothesis
- **This is the canonical "direct probe on the same labels" baseline in streaming form.** A head on a fine-tuned LM predicts the final-sequence label from every prefix. That is already a forecasting target ("will the full output be harmful?").
  - Our observer should be compared against exactly this: a per-prefix value head (linear probe or fine-tuned head) trained with final-label BCE at all positions, with cumulative-max scheduling.
- **Label construction**: "propagate the final label to every prefix" is the cheapest labeling scheme. Its known flaw (StreamGuard's critique) is that generic opening tokens of a harmful response get labeled unsafe.
  - For a hazard/event-time observer we need onset times. These could come from per-prefix judging, FineHarm sentence labels, or MC rollouts.
- **Evaluation protocol to copy (App. A.4)**: truncate at the first trigger and judge whether the shown prefix already contains harmful content (a "leak rate").
  - Generalize this to a lead-time distribution: tokens between the first trigger and the onset of harm.
  - Report TPR at fixed FPR on benign long-form traffic (WildChat, OR-Bench).
- **Horizon realism**: Constitutional Classifier targets are long, detailed CBRN protocols. Example: the output in Fig. 2 is a multi-step lab procedure, and rubrics have about 12 topics. Harmful *detail* accumulates over hundreds of tokens, so "how much harmful detail will this answer eventually cover" (rubric coverage over the remaining response) is a natural long-horizon forecasting target that fits the "what will a long answer cover" framing.
- The cumulative-max ω schedule and the NTP regularizer are cheap tricks to include in both the observer and the baseline.
