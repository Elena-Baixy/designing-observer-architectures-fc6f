# 2604.22266 — Large Language Models Decide Early and Explain Later (Datta, Zhao, Verma, Mamidi, Marreddy, Mehler; preprint Apr 2026, ICML 2026 template)

## Research question
When during chain-of-thought (CoT) is a reasoning model's final answer actually determined? If it is fixed early, how many post-decision tokens are generated, and can a probe on hidden states detect the "answer has stabilized" point for early stopping?

## Method
- **Forced answer completion** (after Lanham et al. 2023): after each reasoning sentence R_i, build prompt `C<think>R1..Ri</think>P` (P = task-specific answer prefix) and read off the answer A_i. This gives an answer trajectory T = (A0..An). Reasoning unit = sentence.
- Trajectory metrics:
  - Final answer switch index t* = max{i : A_i != A_{i+1}} (t* = -1 if the answer never changes).
  - AnswerSwitches = sum_i [A_i != A_{i+1}].
  - Transient Answer Flips (TAFs): deviations of length <= k (k=3) that revert to the previous answer.
  - T_after = T_total - T_{t*+1}, i.e. tokens generated after the final switch.
- **Hold-for-k causal smoothing** (Eq. 4): a new answer is accepted only once it has held for k consecutive steps. The smoother is causal, so it works for streaming monitoring.
- **Early-stopping gate**: m_i = M(C, R1..Ri) in [0,1]; stop at s = min{i : m_i >= tau}; sweeping tau traces a tokens-saved vs. accuracy-drop frontier. Gates compared: random gate (m_i ~ U(0,1)), task-specific probe, and a generic probe trained on the union of all tasks.

## Probe design
- **Linear logistic probe**: m_i = sigma(w^T h^l_i + b), where h^l_i is the hidden state of the **final token of reasoning step i** at layer l (no pooling).
- Labels (App. E): a step is positive if i > t* (halting would not change the answer) and negative if i <= t*. If t* = -1, all steps are positive. S steps are sampled per trace (S is a hyperparameter). Negatives are oversampled to balance minibatches.
- Optimizer: Adam. LR is swept over {1e-4 ... 1e-1}. The layer is chosen by best validation AP. Splits are at the example level (train/val/test).

## Models
Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-14B, Qwen3-30B-A3B (MoE), GPT-OSS-20B (High/Medium reasoning). Temperature 0.6. Query encoder for comparing search queries: sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (cosine >= 0.9 counts as the same query).

## Datasets (App. A sample sizes)
- MCQ: MMLU-Pro (Wang et al. 2024), 1000 traces; the correct option plus 3 distractors are sampled.
- Numeric-answer: PRM800K / "Let's verify step by step" (Lightman 2023), 500 traces, restricted to single-token answers.
- Search-query: RepLiQA (Monteiro 2024) with a search-tool interface, 1000 traces.
- Tool-selection: HF `quotientai/limbic-eval-tool-use-mcp`, 1000 traces.
- Harder sets (App. F): Humanity's Last Exam, GPQA-Diamond, AIME 2026.

## Metrics
Pr(t* = -1), AnswerSwitches, TAFs, T_after (tokens and % of reasoning), accuracy drop vs. tokens saved, final-query cosine similarity (search). Probe layer selection uses AP.

## Key numbers
- Qwen3-4B, averaged over tasks: the answer changes in only **32%** of queries, and **~760 tokens** are generated after the final switch (up to ~1000).
- Table 1, T_after raw/denoised:
  - MCQ Qwen3-4B: 555 (35.7%) / 867 (43.1%).
  - Numeric Qwen3-4B: 847 (42.6%) / 1193 (51.8%).
  - Numeric Qwen3-30B-A3B denoised: 1830 (70.2%).
  - Overall, ~50% of reasoning tokens come after the final answer.
- No-switch fraction: MCQ ~50–68%; numeric 8–31%; tool selection 87–99% (Pr(t* = -1)).
- Denoising cuts mean switches on numeric from ~8 to ~2. TAFs are ~3 per trace on MCQ and numeric.
- Hard sets (raw/denoised):
  - HLE: 10.8 / 1.3 switches; T_after 1186 (49.9%) / 1765 (67.1%).
  - GPQA-D: T_after 1608 / 2259 tokens.
  - AIME26: T_after 660 (16.4%) / 1654 (32.9%).
  - Across these sets, 32–67% of reasoning comes after the final switch.
- Early stopping, Qwen3-4B:
  - The probe gate saves **500 tokens with <2% accuracy drop**. At 1000 tokens saved on numeric, the drop is ~4%.
  - MCQ task-specific probe: 483 tokens saved (37.6%) at a 1.4% drop, vs. random: 513 tokens at a 4.5% drop.
  - Search: the generic probe keeps cosine ~0.98 at 100 tokens saved and >0.95 at 200, vs. random <0.93.
  - The generic probe roughly matches task-specific probes.
- No consistent trend with model size.

## Limitations
- Answers are short and discrete: MCQ letters, single-token numbers, tool names.
- The forced-completion "answer" is a proxy for belief.
- Only a linear single-layer probe at single positions, with no memory.
- Not tested on much longer traces or larger models.
- The probe predicts "the answer has stabilized", which is not the identity of the future answer or the time to an event.

## Relevance to observer/forecasting hypothesis
- **A ready-made event-time setup.** t* is a natural event time per trace, with T_after at hundreds to ~2000 tokens. The probe label "i > t*" is a discretized survival target. A hazard head could predict P(final switch at step j | no switch yet), with traces where t* = -1 treated as events at step 0. Hold-for-k smoothing defines event onset robustly.
- **A baseline to beat.** The linear last-token probe (single layer, chosen by val AP) is exactly the "direct probe" baseline. An observer with trajectory memory should help most where answers flip (numeric: ~1.6 denoised switches), since a memoryless probe cannot tell transient from sustained flips.
- **Forced answer completion is a cheap labeling oracle** for "what will the model eventually output", at every prefix.
- **Evidence the future is linearly decodable early.** The final answer is often fixed hundreds of tokens before it is emitted, which supports long-lead forecasting being feasible.
- **Borrowable metrics:** tokens saved vs. accuracy drop frontiers, and AP for gate quality.
